I am an Assistant Computer Scientist in the Mathematics and Computer Science (MCS) Division at Argonne National Laboratory, working with Dr. Sandeep Madireddy. Before that, I was a postdoctoral appointee at Argonne (2022–2025) with Dr. Sandeep Madireddy and Dr. Prasanna Balaprakash.

I received my Ph.D. in Computer Engineering from Drexel University in 2022, advised by Dr. Anup Das in the Distributed, Intelligent and Scalable Computing (DISCO) Lab. My thesis, Hardware-Software Co-Design for Neuromorphic Computing, developed compilers, mapping tools, and simulators for running spiking neural networks efficiently on neuromorphic hardware. Along the way I interned with Western Digital's neuromorphic team, and before graduate school I spent three years as an embedded engineer at Robert Bosch in India.

My research interests include neuromorphic computing, spiking neural networks, high-performance computing, and, more recently, large reasoning models and multi-modal models: in particular, how test-time compute budgets affect the safety and skill of reasoning models.

Software

NeuroXplorer 1.02020 – 2022
An extensible framework for modeling neuromorphic architectures from a generalized template. Supports cycle-accurate architectural simulation and high-level dataflow analysis, with an optimization engine that weighs hardware metrics (energy, throughput, latency) against SNN metrics such as inter-spike interval distortion and spike disorder.
SpiNeMap2019 – 2020
A design methodology for mapping SNNs to crossbar-based neuromorphic hardware while minimizing spike latency and energy. It partitions an SNN into synapse clusters (SpiNeCluster) and then places those clusters onto hardware (SpiNePlacer).
PyCARL2018 – 2020
A PyNN-based Python interface to CARLsim, the GPU-accelerated SNN simulator, for hardware-software co-simulation. It integrates cycle-accurate models of neuromorphic hardware such as DYNAP-SE to estimate latency and energy.
